# Lessons

What a person needs to understand to have written this application: the
physics it models, the algorithms it runs, and the languages and platform it is
built on. This file is the syllabus. It says which lessons exist or are to be
written, why each one matters to the application, what to read first, and where
in the code the idea lives.

## Two kinds of lesson

**Concept lessons** are written from this syllabus for a reader who has not done
the work. Each supplies its own concrete case first — a worked example with real
Kerbal numbers — then states the general idea, then shows the code that carries
it, and ends with a few questions to answer from memory and one thing to try in
the repository. They are living documents: revised when the code moves, filed
without a hash, with an _as of_ line inside naming the commit they were last
checked against.

**Moment lessons** are written by the `teach-lesson` skill right after a piece of
non-trivial work, for the person who just did it. They are short, dated by the
commit hash in their filename, and never revised. They are case studies; where
one illustrates a concept below, it links to that concept's lesson.

Both kinds file under the same tree, `docs/lessons/<category>/<topic>/`, with the
categories `physics`, `solver`, `renderer`, `ui`, `architecture`,
`verification`, `part-data` and `typescript`. The tree is for browsing by
principle. The strands below are the reading order across it.

## How to read the tables

Each row is one lesson. _Because_ says what the application could not do
without the idea — that is the reason to learn it. _Needs_ lists the lessons to
read first. _Where_ names the code. A ★ marks the core: the rows to read if you
read nothing else.

## Strand 1 — Physics

The rocket, the air it climbs through, and the orbits it flies once it is out.

| #    | Lesson                                                 | Because                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Needs    | Where                                                        |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| P1★  | Δv and the rocket equation                             | Every stage in the tool is sized by solving Tsiolkovsky for the propellant that closes a Δv target; without it there is no stage to size.                                                                | —        | `propellantFor` in `src/core/performance.ts`                 |
| P2★  | Specific impulse, and how it falls with pressure       | An engine's thrust and mass flow at altitude come from its `atmosphereCurve`, and a sea-level Isp used all the way up mis-sizes every first stage by hundreds of m/s.                                    | P1       | `ispAt`, `ispCurve` in `src/core/performance.ts`             |
| P3★  | Thrust-to-weight, and the burn-time limits             | A stage that cannot lift itself, or that burns so long gravity eats the gain, is rejected before anything is flown; the floor is what makes the closed-form search honest.                               | P1       | `solveStage` checks in `src/core/solver.ts`                  |
| P4★  | Staging: why more stages, and when to stop             | The solver walks stage counts from two to six and splits the Δv between them; the mass ratio argument is why the walk exists and why it stops at six.                                                    | P1       | `solveGroup`, `splitShares` in `src/core/solver.ts`          |
| P5★  | The mission Δv budget: legs, margin, and cuts          | A route is a sum of legs with a margin, cut into groups that are solved separately; every number the brief shows descends from this sum.                                                                 | P1       | `buildRoute`, `routeFor` in `src/core/orbits.ts`             |
| P6★  | Gravity, drag and steering losses                      | An ascent to a 2,300 m/s orbit costs about 3,400 m/s, and the difference is what the simulator exists to measure; the closed form only guesses it.                                                       | P1, P3   | `flyAscent` output in `src/core/ascent.ts`                   |
| P7   | The atmosphere as a spline                             | Pressure, density and the speed of sound at every altitude come from per-body curves, and drag and Isp both read them; a wrong density at 10 km moves every predicted ascent.                            | P2       | `makeAtmo`, `atmoFor` in `src/core/atmosphere.ts`            |
| P8★  | Drag in KSP: drag cubes and the Cd chain               | Frontal area comes from measured drag cubes, not nominal diameters, and the Cd chain has five curves; a light probe loses more to drag than to gravity, so this decides which small rockets reach orbit. | P7       | `cdOf`, `frontalArea` in `src/core/atmosphere.ts`            |
| P9★  | The gravity turn                                       | The flown ascent is a kick at a speed and an angle, then prograde; the two parameters are the whole turn search and the reason it can be searched at all.                                                | P6       | `flyAscent`, `optimiseTurn` in `src/core/ascent.ts`          |
| P10  | Why low thrust needs the nose above prograde           | Near TWR 1 following prograde lofts and stalls; the third turn parameter is what let the simulator stop answering "go straight up" and fixed a 44% miss against the game.                                | P9       | `lead`, `LEADS` in `src/core/ascent.ts`                      |
| P11  | Max-Q                                                  | The turn is capped at 40 kPa of dynamic pressure; without the cap the search finds trajectories that are cheap on paper and tear the rocket apart.                                                       | P8, P9   | `optimiseTurn` in `src/core/ascent.ts`                       |
| P12  | Solids: burning through cutoff                         | A solid cannot be shut down, so its thrust limiter is set at build time and it burns past the cutoff the liquids obey; the core throttle search exists to land burnout near apoapsis.                    | P9       | `flyAscent`, `limit` in `src/core/ascent.ts`                 |
| P13★ | Vis-viva, circular speed, and circularising            | The coast to apoapsis is Keplerian and the circularisation burn is integrated, not treated as an impulse; the flown cost of finishing the orbit is what a stage is finally charged.                      | P1       | `vCirc`, coast in `src/core/ascent.ts`, `src/core/orbits.ts` |
| P14★ | Kepler's equation: where a body is at time t           | Every window, encounter and drawing asks where a planet is on a date, and the answer is Newton's method on M = E − e·sin E.                                                                              | —        | `eccentricAnomaly`, `stateAt` in `src/core/kepler.ts`        |
| P15  | Orbital elements and reference frames                  | The stock elements are given at epoch zero in the parent's frame; turning them into positions the Lambert solver can use is a change of frame, and getting a sign wrong mirrors the solar system.        | P14      | `elements`, `toFrame` in `src/core/kepler.ts`                |
| P16★ | Patched conics and the sphere of influence             | The whole transfer model is a chain of two-body problems joined at sphere boundaries; where the handover happens decides what a departure costs.                                                         | P13, P14 | `soiR`, `chainOf` in `src/core/orbits.ts`                    |
| P17★ | The Hohmann transfer and the synodic period            | The cheapest two-burn transfer and how often it recurs are the first estimate of every leg and the span every window search scans.                                                                       | P13, P16 | `hohmann`, `transferDv` in `src/core/orbits.ts`              |
| P18★ | Ejection: characteristic energy and the hyperbolic leg | Leaving a body is priced from the energy C3, which is signed; pricing it from an excess velocity floored at zero threw away every bound departure to a moon and put escape velocity in its place.        | P16, P17 | `c3Of`, `injectC3`, `ejection` in `src/core/transfer.ts`     |
| P19  | Plane change and inclination                           | Targets are inclined to the ecliptic, and the tilt is paid either at a node or from the parking orbit; a one-burn ejection formula assumes the tilt is already gone.                                     | P15, P18 | `relInc`, `planeChanges` in `src/core/orbits.ts`             |
| P20  | The Oberth effect                                      | A burn deep in a well buys more energy per m/s than the same burn high up; it is why departures leave from low orbit and why the ejection formula has the shape it has.                                  | P18      | `injectC3` in `src/core/transfer.ts`                         |
| P21★ | Lambert's problem                                      | Given two positions and a time of flight, the orbit between them is what prices every window cell; Izzo's method is how it is solved fast enough to fill a plot.                                         | P14, P15 | `src/core/lambert.ts`                                        |
| P22★ | Transfer windows and the porkchop plot                 | A window is the minimum of a Δv surface over departure date and flight time; the plot is that surface, and the search over it is what the brief reports as a date.                                       | P21      | `findWindow` in `src/core/transfer.ts`, `porkchop.tsx`       |
| P23  | Encounters on the way                                  | A transfer that passes inside another body's sphere is a different flight; every window is checked against every body and a fouled optimum is stepped off.                                               | P16, P22 | `encountersOf` in `src/core/encounter.ts`                    |
| P24  | Three geometries for moons                             | Inside one system, out to your own moon, and down to the body you are circling are priced differently; two of them have no ejection at all.                                                              | P18, P22 | `findWindow` branches in `src/core/transfer.ts`              |
| P25  | Landing and ascent elsewhere                           | Legs on and off other bodies are tabulated or computed from surface gravity and atmosphere; the route cannot be priced without them.                                                                     | P5, P13  | `ascentLeg`, `landLeg` in `src/core/orbits.ts`               |

## Strand 2 — Algorithms and the solver

How a search over rockets is made small enough to run on a phone, and the
numerical methods under it.

| #    | Lesson                                                      | Because                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Needs    | Where                                                                    |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A1★  | The shape of the search: closed form first, simulation last | Flying a rocket costs milliseconds and sizing one costs microseconds, so the solver sizes millions and flies a handful; the whole architecture follows from that ratio.                                             | P1, P4   | `docs/optimiser-flow.mermaid`, `src/core/solver.ts`                      |
| A2★  | Grid search, then a seeded local refinement                 | The turn optimiser scans a coarse grid and refines round its best; the pattern recurs in the window search, and knowing when a grid is fine enough is what keeps the phone solve under a second.                    | P9       | `optimiseTurn` in `src/core/ascent.ts`                                   |
| A3★  | Bracketing and root finding                                 | Bisection sizes the core of a boosted stage, Newton solves Kepler, golden-section finds the window minimum; each was chosen for a reason, and a bracket that does not contain the answer converges on nonsense.     | P14      | `boostedAscent`, `eccentricAnomaly`, `goldenMin`                         |
| A4★  | Semi-implicit Euler at 0.1 s                                | The ascent is integrated by the simplest stable scheme; why it is stable where explicit Euler is not, and why 0.1 s, decides whether the flown numbers can be trusted to a percent.                                 | P6       | `flyAscent` in `src/core/ascent.ts`                                      |
| A5   | Hermite curves: KSP's FloatCurve                            | Every Isp and atmosphere curve is a cubic Hermite spline with per-key tangents, evaluated exactly as the game does; a linear interpolation is off by a few percent in the places that matter.                       | P2, P7   | `evalCurve` in `src/core/atmosphere.ts`                                  |
| A6★  | Greedy top-down staging, and when greed is wrong            | Stages are solved from the top so each one's mass is the next one's payload; that is right for mass and wrong for cost, and the fix is to solve the compounding objective too.                                      | P4       | `solveGroup` heuristics in `src/core/solver.ts`                          |
| A7★  | The candidate walk                                          | The closed form ranks candidates and the simulator flies them cheapest first, re-sizing what falls short; how many runners-up to keep and how to rank them is the difference between a good rocket and a cheap one. | A1, A6   | `solveGroup` in `src/core/solver.ts`, `carriedFor` in `src/core/plan.ts` |
| A8   | Packing propellant into tanks                               | A Δv target becomes a tonnage, and a tonnage becomes a list of tank parts by four heuristics and a consolidation pass; the packing is most of a stage's part count and cost.                                        | P1       | `pickTanksRaw` in `src/core/tanks.ts`                                    |
| A9   | Packing circles: clusters and rings                         | Engines cluster under a tank and columns ring a core; the enclosing-circle formula decides whether a cluster fits a diameter and how wide a boosted stage is.                                                       | —        | `clusterSpan`, `ringPositions` in `src/core/geometry.ts`                 |
| A10  | Fitting structure as a graph walk                           | Couplers, adapters, plates, shrouds and decouplers are chosen by walking a graph of sizes from narrow to wide; the walk is shared by the two stage paths and is where most structural bugs have lived.              | A8       | `fitStructure`, `adapterChain` in `src/core/parts.ts`                    |
| A11★ | Memoisation, and what a cache key is                        | One function is called 124 million times in a solve, and the caches are keyed on array identity; a key that is too coarse returns another rocket's answer.                                                          | A1       | `ispAt`, `pickTanksMemo`, `_fitCache`                                    |
| A12★ | Sharding a search across workers                            | The stage-count units run on a pool of workers and fold back deterministically; the fold has to give the same answer in any order or the design depends on the phone.                                               | A1, L5   | `fanOut` in `src/core/plan.ts`, `src/ui/unit.worker.ts`                  |
| A13  | Slenderness as a constraint on the whole rocket             | A rocket that is too tall for its width falls over in the game; measuring the ratio on a segment let the solver build pencils, and treating it as a tie-break let them through.                                     | A9       | chain scoring in `src/core/solver.ts`                                    |
| A14  | The boosted ascent: two phases in closed form               | Boosters, drop tanks and asparagus columns are priced without flying them by splitting the burn into a phase with and a phase without; the split is what makes a hundred booster layouts affordable to try.         | P1, A3   | `boostedAscent`, `boostDv` in `src/core/solver.ts`                       |
| A15  | Objectives, tie-breaks, and the coupling term               | Mass, cost and part count are minimised with a small mass term to break ties; the relations between them — cheapest is never dearer than lightest — are invariants the tests hold.                                  | A6       | `scoreOf`, `better` in `src/core/solver.ts`                              |
| A16  | Placing labels by scored search                             | Names on the transfer drawing are placed by trying candidates most-constrained first and scoring overlap with real text metrics; it is a small constraint-satisfaction problem drawn on a canvas.                   | —        | `place` in `src/ui/components/transfer.tsx`                              |
| A17  | Quadric edge collapse                                       | The engines are the game's meshes simplified to a fraction of their triangles; the metric that chooses which edges to collapse decides whether a bell still reads as a bell.                                        | L14      | `tools/engine-meshes.mjs`, `src/ui/components/three-view.tsx`            |
| A18★ | Hidden-line removal with a surface-id buffer                | The build view draws what is behind the rocket as dashes; an id pass, a depth test and arc length measured on the geometry are how a schematic is made from a solid render.                                         | L12, L13 | `src/ui/components/shaders.ts`, `hidden-lines.ts`                        |
| A19  | Compressing a design into a link                            | The share link deflates the configuration against a default the reader can rebuild, so sixty tech-node names fit in a hash; and it inflates it with a cap, because a link is untrusted input.                       | L1       | `src/ui/link.ts`                                                         |

## Strand 3 — Language and platform

TypeScript, React, the browser, Web Workers, three.js and GLSL: what each one
does that the application leans on.

| #    | Lesson                                                     | Because                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Needs    | Where                                                      |
| ---- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| L1★  | Plain data across a seam                                   | Everything crossing `planMission` is JSON-shaped — no `Set`, `Map`, class or function — which is what lets the solver run on a worker today and in Rust tomorrow; the test that enforces it is the architecture.      | —        | `src/core/plan.ts`, `test/seam-contract.test.ts`           |
| L2★  | Discriminated unions and narrowing                         | A shape is a cylinder or a ring, a part is an engine or a tank, and the compiler checks every branch handles every kind; this is how a model of a rocket is made impossible to half-draw.                             | —        | `src/core/model.ts`, `src/core/parts.ts`                   |
| L3   | Types for measurements                                     | The part tables are JSON typed as readonly records keyed by the strings the data uses; the types say what a number is a measurement of, and stop configuration creeping into data.                                    | L2       | `src/core/parts.ts`, `src/data/*.json`                     |
| L4   | Modules, exports and the import direction                  | Solver functions are private unless a caller needs them, and `core/` may not import `ui/`; a test holds the direction because neither the type checker nor the bundler will.                                          | L1       | `test/boundaries.test.ts`                                  |
| L5★  | Web Workers: messages, cancellation, and structured clone  | The solve runs off the page's thread and can be abandoned mid-search; the protocol, `AbortSignal`, and what structured clone will and will not copy are the whole worker story.                                       | L1       | `src/ui/solver-client.ts`, `solver.worker.ts`              |
| L6   | The main thread and the reply queue                        | Moving work to a worker moves the computation, not the delivery; a reply waits for the page's thread to be free, which is why the finer plot pass had to be scheduled the way it is.                                  | L5       | `src/ui/plot-client.ts`                                    |
| L7★  | React effects, refs, and drafts                            | Effects run after render, a ref installed by one stops working when the element moves, and a text field must render its draft not its value; three facts behind most of the UI's rules file.                          | —        | `src/ui/components/primitives.tsx`, `.claude/rules/ui.md`  |
| L8★  | Inline styles, the CSSOM, and design tokens                | There is no CSS file: a stylesheet is generated from tokens and everything else is `style={{}}`; what the CSSOM accepts, silently discards, and how shorthands interact with React decides what can be styled at all. | —        | `src/ui/tokens.ts`, `styles.ts`, `.claude/rules/design.md` |
| L9   | Layout: flex basis, sticky, and stacking contexts          | The desktop sheet, the phone's scrubber and every overlay depend on three CSS behaviours that are not what their names suggest.                                                                                       | L8       | `src/ui/app.tsx`, `src/ui/components/build.tsx`            |
| L10★ | Accessibility as engineering                               | Focus traps, `:focus-visible`, `aria-hidden` versus `visibility`, live regions and 44 px targets are measured by a suite, not asserted; each is a mechanism with a rule about when it applies.                        | L7       | `useTrap`, `visual/layout.test.ts`                         |
| L11★ | three.js: scene, orthographic camera, and the camera basis | The build view is four orthographic views of one model; how `lookAt` builds a basis and why every camera must send world +x to the right of the screen is what keeps the columns where the solver put them.           | —        | `src/ui/views.ts`, `three-view.tsx`                        |
| L12★ | Render targets and multi-pass rendering                    | The schematic look is three passes into off-screen buffers composited by a full-screen quad; render targets, their formats, and what filtering does to an id buffer are the tools.                                    | L11      | `src/ui/components/shaders.ts`                             |
| L13★ | GLSL: uniforms, varyings, and numbers                      | Shading, ids and dashes are computed per fragment in a language with no integer-to-float promotion and no colour management; a `3` where `3.0` was meant compiles and draws wrong.                                    | L11      | `src/ui/components/shaders.ts`                             |
| L14  | Revolved geometry, normals, creases and silhouettes        | Tanks are lathes and engines are meshes; a crease is a property of the geometry and a silhouette of the view, and the line drawing needs both found differently.                                                      | L11      | `src/ui/components/hidden-lines.ts`                        |
| L15  | Animation clocks and easing                                | A separation and an arrival are functions of time eased at both ends; the frame's timestamp is the frame's start, and the last frame must be the still drawing to the bit or the pixel check cannot pass.             | L7, L11  | `src/ui/separation.ts`, `build.tsx`                        |
| L16  | Canvas sizing and device pixels                            | A WebGL canvas has a buffer size and a CSS size, and resizing it per frame reallocates every render target; the dash period is in CSS pixels scaled to device pixels for the same reason.                             | L11, L12 | `ThreeView` in `three-view.tsx`                            |
| L17  | What the toolchain checks and what it strips               | Vite and vitest strip types with esbuild and never check them, eslint has no TypeScript parser, so `tsc` is the only type gate; knowing which tool sees what decides where a bug can hide.                            | —        | `package.json`, `.github/workflows/build.yml`              |
